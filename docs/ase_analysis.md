# Culture practices by ASE hospitalization status

The additional analysis divides adult ICU hospitalizations into three mutually exclusive groups: **No presumed infection**, **Presumed infection without ASE**, and **ASE**. It runs after the existing whole-cohort analyses and writes aggregate results under `output/runs/<run_id>/ase/`. Sites keep the same four configuration fields and run `Rscript code/00_run_pipeline.R` after restoring the updated lockfile.

## Membership and interpretation

Primary membership is retrospective: a hospitalization is ASE if **any** blood-culture episode during the full hospitalization meets presumed serious infection plus qualifying acute organ dysfunction. An evaluable hospitalization with presumed infection but no primary ASE episode is **Presumed infection without ASE**; one without any presumed-infection episode is **No presumed infection**. ASE takes priority if multiple episodes occur; no hospitalization appears in more than one group. All ICU stays, ICU-days, and cultures from that hospitalization inherit the same label, including observations before the event. This is not a time-varying classification and is not limited to ASE events that begin inside the ICU or the study window. Only ICU exposure and culture outcomes inside the shared study dates are analyzed.

No presumed infection means the surveillance infection criteria were not met; it does not establish absence of infection. Missing source tables/columns or unverified component activity skip the additional stage with an explicit status. Children, missing ages, and invalid hospitalization boundaries are counted in exclusion QC rather than assigned any subgroup. The whole-cohort analysis is unchanged.

ASE includes blood-culture ordering in its definition. Differences in blood-culture collection between these groups are partly built into group membership. Changes in culturing, treatment duration, or organ-function measurement can also change who qualifies. Results describe surveillance-defined populations and should not be interpreted as causal effects of sepsis on culturing or as risk-adjusted site comparisons.

## Definition and reference

Reference: [clifpy ASE implementation](https://github.com/Common-Longitudinal-ICU-data-Format/clifpy/blob/12e16ba9891ea6512fd464859565fdc3ed5ed8da/clifpy/utils/ase.py), pinned at commit `12e16ba9891ea6512fd464859565fdc3ed5ed8da`; [CDC surveillance toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/sepsis/media/pdfs/sepsis-surveillance-toolkit-aug-2018-508.pdf). Attributed SQL and the Apache-2.0 license are stored in `utils/ase/`. R executes the queries using pinned DuckDB/DBI packages; no Python setup is required.

- Presumed infection requires a blood culture (any result) and a new qualifying antimicrobial course starting within ±2 calendar days, including new IV/IM treatment and normally at least four qualifying antimicrobial days. The reference's single-day-gap, drug-switching, oral/IV vancomycin distinction, and short-course death/transfer/hospice censoring logic are retained.
- Primary organ dysfunction excludes lactate, following the linked code's default. It includes new qualifying vasopressor infusion outside procedural care, new invasive ventilation, doubling creatinine excluding ESRD, bilirubin at least 2 mg/dL and doubled, or platelets below 100 × 10³/µL with at least a 50% fall from an eligible baseline.
- Community and hospital laboratory baselines follow the reference queries. Values above the reference outlier caps are excluded. Sites should verify CLIF lab units and clinical mapping before interpreting results. The numeric thresholds expect creatinine/bilirubin in mg/dL, platelets in 10³/µL, and lactate in mmol/L; UCMC’s creatinine, bilirubin, and platelet units were checked against these conventions.
- Lactate-inclusive flags remain in the private classification and the aggregate QC reports additional hospitalizations that would qualify. Separate lactate-inclusive trend analyses are not produced.
- A repeat-infection timeframe changes episode counts, but not whether a hospitalization has any qualifying episode. This analysis does not count ASE episodes and does not apply an episode filter.
- Calendar-day windows and month allocation use the project's UTC timestamp convention, consistently with existing analyses. Sites must ensure their timestamps are correctly standardized.

Documented adaptations from the linked code:

1. Blood-culture anchors require `method_category == culture`, with duplicate hospitalization/collection timestamps collapsed.
2. QAD hospitalization limits compare dates with dates, preserving admission-day treatment and up to two pre-admission calendar days of ED care.
3. Vasopressor and ventilation windows use ±2 **calendar days**, instead of the reference's timestamp-based ±48 hours.
4. Only administered, positive-dose qualifying antimicrobial records and the five CDC vasopressors are used; held/ordered drugs are excluded.
5. ESRD exclusion uses explicit `N18.6`; the broader upstream list includes `I27.2`, which is not an ESRD code.
6. The censoring query additionally recognizes an explicit `comfort_measures_only` discharge category. Unrecorded comfort transitions cannot be inferred.
7. Raw lab values may supply the reference SQL's numeric fallback rather than being discarded by the upstream loading wrapper.

## Required source data

The stage needs `patient`, `hospitalization`, `adt`, `microbiology_culture`, `medication_admin_intermittent`, `medication_admin_continuous`, `labs`, `respiratory_support`, and `hospital_diagnosis`. Full hospitalization data are needed, including baseline labs, antibiotic history, follow-up treatment, and discharge disposition outside the culture study dates. Merely supplying ICU-only extracts is insufficient.

A missing susceptibility table skips only the ASE-stratified susceptibility analysis. Missing ASE inputs never cause every patient to be assigned to a subgroup. Availability statuses, component activity, classification/exclusion QC, and the pinned definition are recorded in the aggregate export.

## Outputs and denominators

- Cohort counts and monthly ICU admissions/ICU-days, including uncultured stays. Carry-in stays contribute ICU-days but not new admissions.
- Monthly culture collection, positive-event rates, positivity, and exact specimen-category summaries. Categories are not selected independently by subgroup or collapsed into different Other bins.
- Admission-level proportion cultured and first-culture timing among new ICU admissions. Each admission's first culture is measured from its own ICU entry; admissions without a culture remain in the proportion denominator.
- Organism distribution, event/category detection counts, and monthly detection rates. Each organism category is counted once per culture event. Rates reflect detection events, including repeat cultures, not incident infections.
- Separate season-adjusted negative-binomial GAMs for culture and organism counts, using each group's ICU-day and ICU-admission offsets. Annualized endpoint contrasts, pointwise curves, residual diagnostics, and BH-adjusted screens use the existing shared modeling code. These are within-group time contrasts, not between-group effect estimates.
- If AST is available: subgroup organism–drug–specimen counts, tested fractions, QC, susceptible/non-susceptible detection models, and quasi-binomial non-susceptibility models using the existing testing/linkage rules.

Both groups share the full study calendar. Months without subgroup exposure have unavailable rates. Organism zero counts can enter models when the subgroup has exposure and the site still has observed culture-source activity. AST testing remains unknown when interpretable results are unavailable; it is never inferred susceptible.

Identifier-bearing hospitalization labels and criteria timestamps stay under ignored `data/intermediate/runs/<run_id>/ase/`. The existing export audit runs after this stage. Current central pooling does not automatically include these subgroup exports; subgroup pooling requires a separate extension.

## Validation

Run `Rscript tests/test_ase.R` for clinical boundary cases and subgroup denominator/count reconciliation. Before buddy release, reconcile samples of hospitalizations from each of the three groups against source records, review lab units, antimicrobial/route/MAR mapping and observation coverage, and confirm exclusion counts. Synthetic fixtures validate implementation behavior, not clinical accuracy at a site.

## Subgroup demographics and characteristics

`ase_characteristics_table1_*.csv` provides a side-by-side Table 1 for all three groups. `ase_characteristics_long_*.csv` contains numeric counts, percentages, medians and quartiles for reuse across sites. Both are automatically included in the site export manifest. `ase_characteristics_qc_*.csv` reports hospitalization totals, distinct patients overall and patients represented in multiple groups.

The analysis unit is one classified adult hospitalization with ICU time overlapping the study window, including hospitalizations without cultures. Demographics are age at hospitalization admission and standardized patient sex, race and ethnicity categories. Characteristics include admission type, discharge disposition, full hospital length of stay, ICU days and number of ICU stays overlapping the study window, ICU culture counts and whether any ICU culture or positive culture was collected. Hospital length of stay and discharge disposition describe the completed hospitalization; they are not baseline severity measures. ICU and culture measures cover only study-window ICU time.

Categorical percentages use all hospitalizations in each subgroup as the denominator, including missing/unknown values shown as a separate row. Continuous summaries are median [25th, 75th percentile] among observed values. Every characteristic includes observed and missing counts; absent optional demographic columns are explicitly marked `source_column_unavailable`. Missing, unknown or empty source labels are treated as missing. Patients with multiple hospitalizations contribute once per hospitalization and may appear in multiple groups; subgroup patient counts should not be added to obtain a unique cohort count. No row-level identifiers or dates are exported. These are descriptive comparisons without hypothesis tests or causal interpretation.

Additional clinical descriptors report in-hospital death (`discharge_category = expired`), recorded IMV (`device_category = imv`) and recorded nonprocedural vasopressor use (administered positive-dose norepinephrine, dopamine, epinephrine, phenylephrine or vasopressin). Support observations must fall within admission-inclusive/discharge-exclusive hospitalization boundaries. These are whole-hospitalization recorded treatments, not baseline variables or ASE-specific organ dysfunction: no blood-culture proximity or new-initiation requirement is applied. A “no” indicates no qualifying recorded treatment, conditional on the available source tables; it does not prove complete chart capture. Missing discharge disposition leaves mortality unknown. These measures can overlap the criteria used to classify ASE and should be interpreted descriptively.

The exported `ase_group` column retains its existing name and now contains the three labels above. Outputs in a fresh run replace the previous two-group comparison; results from earlier runs retain their original definitions. All three groups receive their own demographics, ICU denominators, culture/specimen rates, positivity, timing, organism distributions and detection models, and optional susceptibility analyses. Presumed infection without ASE is not the full presumed-infection cohort: the latter is the union of that group and ASE. Unique patients may overlap groups across hospitalizations, and their group counts are not additive. Plot colors and group order are consistent across outputs.

`ase_cohort_reconciliation_*.csv` checks the sum across the three groups against the eligible cohort for hospitalizations, ICU stays, ICU-days, culture events and positive culture events. A mismatch fails the stage; excluded/unclassifiable hospitalizations are reported separately.

## Cumulative collection and detection

Each group has observed cumulative curves from ICU admission for first culture and first positive culture (default 0–14 days), first detection of each standardized organism category (default 0–168 hours), and all culture events by exact specimen category (default 0–168 hours). Five `ase_cumulative_*.csv` exports contain first-event counts, organism detection counts, specimen event counts, observation counts and denominator QC. Three corresponding plots compare the groups. Organism/specimen plots use common site-wide selections across groups; numeric exports retain all categories. Organism detection time is specimen collection time, not result availability or confirmed infection onset.

Denominators are all new ICU admissions in each subgroup, including uncultured admissions. Carry-in stays that began before the study window are excluded from these time-origin analyses. ICU events remain restricted to study-window half-open ICU intervals, and follow-up ends at ICU exit or study end. First events at exactly a day/hour endpoint are included; fractional hours are assigned to the next integer-hour endpoint. Repeat detections count once per ICU admission and organism for first-detection curves, whereas culture accumulation includes every distinct culture event and can exceed 100 events per 100 admissions.

These are observed cumulative proportions, not Kaplan–Meier estimates or competing-risk-adjusted cumulative incidence functions. Admissions remain in the fixed denominator after ICU exit; no further ICU events accrue after exit. `n_icu_admissions_under_observation` reports admissions whose observed ICU stay extends beyond each endpoint, including admissions already cultured. This is an observation count, not the first-event risk set. Study-end truncation is not corrected. Zero-size subgroups have unavailable proportions rather than artificial zero risk. These curves describe surveillance-defined groups and do not estimate a causal effect of ASE. Existing `TIMING_MAX_ICU_DAY`, `TIMING_MAX_ICU_HOUR`, `TOP_N_TIMING_ORGANISMS` and `TOP_N_CULTURE_TYPES` overrides control the horizons and displayed categories; no new site configuration is required.

## Clinical timing and incremental yield

**ASE event timing:** one first event per ASE hospitalization is described using two independent anchors: the first qualifying blood-culture collection and the earliest qualifying non-lactate organ-dysfunction timestamp across qualifying anchors. The latter is an onset proxy, not clinical recognition or a prospectively available diagnosis. No antimicrobial-course midnight date is used as an exact clinical onset time. Full-hospitalization merged ICU intervals classify first anchors before first ICU entry, within the first 48 hours of a matched ICU stay, at 48 hours through 7 days, after 7 days, between stays, or after last exit. ICU exit is exclusive. First-event day distributions use matched-stay day 1 for hours [0,24), with day 15 and later combined. The denominator remains all ASE hospitalizations; separate flags show whether the first anchor falls within study dates. Admission-level exports separately show whether the first anchor occurred at/before each study-cohort ICU entry, during that stay, or after it. These are retrospective first-event descriptions; raw qualifying blood-culture anchors can overlap and are not counted as independent ASE episodes. Subsequent independent ASE episodes are not defined here.

**Antibiotic-relative cultures:** collection is compared with the first recorded administered, positive-dose CMS-sepsis-qualifying IV/IM dose within the hospitalization, allowing two pre-admission calendar days of ED treatment. Categories distinguish before, identical recorded timestamps, after, and no recorded qualifying dose. Exact simultaneous timestamps do not establish ordering. Additional strata distinguish no dose in the preceding 24 hours, recent drug-course starts within 48 hours, and ongoing courses starting earlier. Prior-dose windows are collection-minus-24-hours exclusive through collection inclusive; course starts use the reference's drug-specific gap of more than two calendar days. These are recorded exposure measures, not all antibiotic use, adherence scores or causal estimates. Positivity and collection fractions are exported by group and exact specimen category; the main comparison plot focuses on blood. Drug changes and antibiotic exposure can help define ASE membership, so comparisons remain descriptive.

**Repeat-culture yield:** simultaneous collections with the same standardized specimen category, ICU stay and collection timestamp are collapsed. Within each stay/specimen, a collection episode starts at the first collection; later collections within 72 hours belong to that fixed index episode, and the next collection after 72 hours starts a new episode. Nested 24-, 48- and 72-hour windows include their endpoints. These are collection episodes, not validated blood-culture sets or infection episodes. Repeats are stratified by the index result and classified as negative/no growth, mixed/contaminated, indeterminate, the same mapped organism category only, or a mapped organism category absent from the index; unmapped positive results have explicit uncertain categories. Novelty is relative to the index, not a claim of a new infection or strain. Index result availability at repeat collection uses the latest recorded result timestamp only when all index rows have timestamps. This may reflect final/updated rather than preliminary results and cannot prove what clinicians knew. Denominators are repeat collections in the group/specimen/index-result/window stratum. Nested windows and availability strata should not be summed across windows. No appropriateness claim is made.

**Early/later ICU comparisons:** first 48 hours are [entry, entry+48h); exactly 48 hours belongs to the later phase. Collection timing does not establish ICU-acquired infection. Each phase has its own study-window ICU-day denominator and number of ICU stays exposed to that phase, including uncultured stays. Carry-in stays contribute observed phase time according to original ICU entry. Specimen yield and organism event detection rates are exported for each group/phase; first organism detections per ICU stay are also distinguished from repeated detections. At sites with AST, phase-specific monthly susceptibility counts, fractions, coverage/eligibility and QC use the existing linkage and testing thresholds. Rates use phase-specific ICU-days; untested isolates never establish susceptibility. No additional phase-specific temporal model is fitted.

These extensions produce `ase_culture_antibiotic_timing`, `ase_culture_recent_antibiotic_exposure`, `ase_repeat_culture_yield/qc`, `ase_early_later_*`, and `ase_event_*`/`ase_first_event_relative_to_icu_admissions` aggregate CSVs plus plots. Row-level comparisons and timestamps stay in private intermediates. Existing site configuration is sufficient. Run `tests/test_ase_clinical.R` and the full synthetic integration test before release.

ASE timing QC explicitly checks coverage of the ASE hospitalization cohort for both anchors. Location summaries also report median and quartiles of elapsed hours from first ICU entry, allowing negative values for pre-ICU anchors. First organism detections in early/later outputs are first observed detections within the study-window ICU data, which may omit earlier pre-window detections in carry-in stays.

`ase_clinical_reconciliation` verifies that antibiotic strata and early/later strata preserve culture counts, phase ICU-days sum to the eligible ICU exposure, and collection episodes plus repeats account for every collapsed collection timestamp. A mismatch fails the analysis stage.

Antibiotic positivity and incremental-repeat-yield plots display only strata with at least 20 culture events or repeat collections, respectively, to avoid tiny cells dominating visual comparisons. This is a presentation rule, not an analysis exclusion or statistical significance threshold; all numeric strata remain in the CSV exports. Antibiotic timing preserves fractional-second differences when present in source timestamps. Early/later organism-rate plots use the same ten most frequently detected categories across groups.

Repeat-yield exports contain both `repeat_yield` (relative to the index collection) and `incremental_yield` (relative to the union of mapped organism categories from every earlier collection in the same 72-hour episode). Thus a repeatedly recovered organism after an initially negative index may be absent from the index yet provide no new organism information beyond the first positive repeat. The incremental-yield figure uses the latter definition. Prior unmapped positive organisms make novelty explicitly uncertain; result timing remains a separate recorded-availability stratum, so detection novelty does not establish information newly available to clinicians at the moment of collection.

### Culture timing density and counts

Overlaid curves compare the three hospitalization groups for all cultures and blood cultures during the first 168 ICU hours (or the existing timing horizon override). All culture events, including repeats, contribute; carry-in ICU stays are excluded as for the cumulative curves. The density uses the privacy-safe hourly event counts, approximating nonzero ceiling-hour bins by midpoints, with a common 6-hour Gaussian bandwidth and reflection at zero and the displayed horizon. Each nonempty group/specimen curve integrates to one within the displayed horizon; empty groups have unavailable densities. Density describes collection timing conditional on a collection in the displayed interval, not culture volume or an ICU-hour-adjusted collection rate. Companion plots and CSV exports show actual counts in consecutive 6-hour bins (including any shorter final bin); these retain differences in volume but reflect subgroup size and duration of observation. Numeric density/count exports retain every group.

The overlaid one-hour histogram and `ase_culture_timing_histogram_1h` CSV retain actual event counts for all cultures and blood cultures. Bins preserve the cumulative-table ceiling-hour convention: `[0,1]`, `(1,2]`, and so on, including exact-entry collections in the first bin. Transparent bars overlap rather than stack. Zero-count bins and absent groups are retained.

Additional one-hour histogram pages display every nonempty non-blood standardized specimen category, ordered by total volume, with nine panels per page and independent count scales. Categories are not combined (including general versus lower respiratory tract). `ase_specimen_histogram_1h` retains all source categories and zero-count bins for all three groups.

### Optional onset-aligned preview

`Rscript code/12_preview_ase_onset.R RUN_ID` uses the completed run's private first-event and qualifying blood-anchor tables with the site's parquet hospitalization and culture sources. It writes aggregate-only CSVs and figures under `output/review_figures/RUN_ID/ase_onset_preview_1h`; it is currently an optional preview rather than a default pipeline stage. One earliest qualifying non-lactate organ-dysfunction proxy per ASE hospitalization is aligned to zero, with one-hour bins across `[-48,72)` hours. The two groups are proxy at/before first ICU entry and proxy during an ICU stay; first proxies between stays or after last ICU exit are reported in QC and excluded from these two panels.

Unlike ICU-admission histograms, this preview includes full-hospitalization cultures collected inside hospitalization boundaries and study dates, including non-ICU collections and repeats, using shared event/result collapsing and specimen overrides. Collection rates use observed hospitalized hours intersected with each relative-time bin and study dates, including patients without a culture in that bin. Positivity is positive events divided by all collected events, including mixed and indeterminate results; display requires at least 20 collections, with all numeric counts retained. Eventual culture results are assigned to collection time, not preliminary result availability. Sensitivity figures remove every blood culture event at the timestamp of the qualifying blood anchor linked to the earliest organ-dysfunction proxy. This reduces the defining-collection contribution but does not remove selection dependence of the retrospective ASE definition. These descriptive results are not estimates of clinical recognition time or causal treatment effects.
